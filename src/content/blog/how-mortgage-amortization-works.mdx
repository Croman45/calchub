If you've ever looked at a mortgage statement and wondered why so little of your early payments
seem to go toward your actual balance, you've bumped into amortization - the process that
determines exactly how each payment splits between interest and principal over the life of a
loan.

## The core idea

Every fixed-rate mortgage payment is the same dollar amount from your first payment to your
last. What changes, month to month, is the *mix* of that payment:

- Early on, most of your payment covers **interest** on the (still large) remaining balance.
- Later, most of your payment reduces **principal**, since the balance - and the interest charged
  on it - has shrunk.

This is why paying off a 30-year mortgage feels slow at first: in year one, you might pay
thousands in interest for every thousand dollars of principal you actually pay down.

## The formula

The monthly payment itself is constant, calculated once at the start of the loan:

```
M = P × [r(1+r)^n] / [(1+r)^n − 1]
```

Where `P` is the loan principal, `r` is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and `n` is
the total number of payments (years × 12). Our [Mortgage Calculator](/finance/mortgage) applies
this exact formula, then adds your property tax and insurance on top so the total matches what
you'd see on a real monthly statement.

### A worked example

Take a $400,000 home with 20% down ($80,000), a 30-year term, and a 6.5% rate:

1. Loan principal: $400,000 − $80,000 = $320,000
2. Monthly rate: 6.5% ÷ 12 ≈ 0.542%
3. Number of payments: 30 × 12 = 360
4. Applying the formula gives a principal & interest payment of roughly $2,023/month

In month one, roughly $1,733 of that payment is interest and only about $290 reduces principal.
By month 300 (25 years in), that ratio has almost completely flipped.

## Why extra payments matter so much early on

Because so much of an early payment is interest, any extra amount you pay toward principal in
the first few years compounds in your favor for the rest of the loan - it reduces the balance
that all future interest is calculated on. This is why even a modest extra principal payment
early in a 30-year mortgage can shave years off the loan and save a significant amount in total
interest.
