Body Mass Index (BMI) shows up on medical charts, insurance forms, and fitness apps everywhere,
but few people know where it came from or what it was actually designed to measure.

## What BMI actually calculates

BMI is a simple ratio of weight to height, squared:

```
BMI = weight (kg) / height (m)²
```

That's it - no age, no sex, no muscle mass, no body fat percentage. It was developed in the
1830s by a Belgian mathematician, Adolphe Quetelet, as a way to describe *population-level*
trends in body size, not to diagnose any individual's health.

## The standard categories

Most health organizations, including the World Health Organization, use these BMI ranges for
adults:

- **Below 18.5** - Underweight
- **18.5 - 24.9** - Normal weight
- **25 - 29.9** - Overweight
- **30 and above** - Obesity

## Where BMI falls short

Because BMI only looks at weight and height, it can't distinguish between muscle and fat. A
bodybuilder and a sedentary person of the same height and weight will show an identical BMI,
despite having very different body compositions and health profiles. BMI also tends to be less
reliable for:

- Athletes and highly muscular individuals
- Older adults, who naturally lose muscle mass with age
- Pregnant people
- Children and teenagers (who need age- and sex-specific growth charts instead)

## A better picture: pairing BMI with body fat percentage

Because of these limitations, BMI is best used as a quick, free screening tool - not a final
verdict. Pairing it with a body fat percentage estimate, like the one from our
[Body Fat Calculator](/health/body-fat), gives a much clearer sense of body composition, since it
directly estimates how much of your weight is fat versus lean mass.

## The takeaway

BMI is useful precisely because it's simple: no equipment beyond a scale and a tape measure, and
a formula anyone can compute in seconds. Just don't treat a single BMI number as a complete
health verdict - use it as one data point among several, and talk to a doctor about what's
healthy for your specific body and circumstances.
